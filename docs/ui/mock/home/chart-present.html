At Anchor — one sheet: the voyage above, her plan below

The chart got the room and the ship paid for it: Home is one sheet of parchment, the voyage above and her plan below.

The chart got the room, and she paid for it. Home is one sheet of parchment floating on the sea. The voyage is inked across its top two-thirds (1156×272, roughly twice the area her plan gets); her own drawing is ruled along its foot (756×164), with one waterline rule between her weather deck and her hold. There is no tab, no chart_raise, no scrim, no mid-flip frame where input is swallowed. There is also no second state to be in, which is the point: today the answer to "where am I and where can I go" costs a click, a tween and a modal that hides the ship you were just working on.

What the constraint forced, and it is not a small thing: she stops being a picture. The reason the shipped screen can afford to hide the chart is that the ship is the screen — a lit three-quarter cutaway filling 1244×700, whose berths are picked as projected quads on her hull (galleon_room_at). Those quads are already small. Give half the screen away and they stop being hittable; there is no version of this layout where the rendered galleon shrinks and the refit still works. So the ship on this screen is a plan, in the chart's own ink, on the chart's own paper — and the berths get bigger as the ship gets smaller. A Small hold is a 139px compartment and a Large one is 190px, at galleon_size_weight's own shares (1.0 / 1.55 / 2.3), so size is still read off the drawing rather than off a word. Exposed above the waterline, Concealed below it: the same geography the 3D cutaway carries, flattened. Paper in a berth means something is fitted; bare timber means it is empty.

The honest cost, stated plainly: the best-looking thing in the game is no longer on its main screen. And draw_build_surface_body's at_home flag stops being a two-line difference between Home and Refit — a Home that is a chart and a Refit that is a cutaway are two screens, not one body with a boolean. Either Refit adopts the plan too, or the pair forks. That is a decision, not a detail, and this mockup is what forces it.

The two destructive acts are separated by ground before they are separated by words. Over the Side is off the paper — a card down in the water past the sheet's bottom edge, which is exactly what dragging a fitting off a ship looks like. Jettison is inside the books, the right-hand cell of the stats strip, because burning a berth's cargo is the one act whose entire cost is a number on that strip. A player can tell them apart without reading either, which the shipped pair (a bin bottom-left, the ledger bottom-centre, both parchment, both washed coral at the same moment) cannot quite claim.

Putting the chart on the refit screen breaks a palette law, and the fix is the most interesting thing here. Coral is reserved, appears once per screen, and currently has two owners: the chart's Haven X, and ui_alarm's destructive drops. Today they never meet, because the chart is a modal. Share one screen and they meet constantly. The resolution drawn above is a lease: at rest the Haven holds the coral; the instant a fitting leaves its berth the lease transfers, the Haven falls back to sepia (which is why its mark is rock-toned in this frame) and coral lights only the two drop targets. One colour, one meaning, at every instant — enforced rather than argued about in a comment.

Three things this frame is honest about being weaker at. The chart is 272px tall against the raised chart's 640: four lanes fit, five would crowd, and a voyage that ever grows a lane wants this layout re-argued rather than squeezed. Long occupant names do not fit a Medium compartment — "Captain's Quarters" measures about 162px at 16px in a 128px berth, which is only invisible here because that fitting happens to be in the player's hand. And the harness cannot draw a diagonal, so the two slanting legs of the fan are blocked in as runs of 4px marks; the game draws them with DrawLineEx and they are not a new capability.

What I would need from presentation/ui.odin (and its widgets) that does not exist yet

  • A chart that takes a rect. draw_map draws at MAP_AREA, a package-level var that map_width_set mutates — width only, with a whole comment explaining that each screen must re-assert it every frame so hit-testing agrees with drawing. This design needs the chart at an arbitrary rect, height included, with the node field re-laid out to match. The shape wanted is draw_map(state, area, mouse) and map_node_at(state, area, point), taking the rect as an argument rather than reading a global. Everything else here is layout; this one is the change that makes the whole idea possible.
  • A coral lease. ui_alarm is documented as the only place coral is drawn as chrome, and it has no idea what else on the screen is spending coral. Something like ui_danger_claim() -> bool / Ui_Danger_Lease, consulted by ui_alarm and by the Haven's mark inside draw_map, so "coral appears once per screen" becomes a thing the widgets enforce. Today the rule survives only because the two owners are on different screens, and this mockup ends that.
  • A plan row. ui_plan_row(rect, sizes) -> []rl.Rectangle — N compartments laid across a rect in proportion to galleon_size_weight, gapped by a bulkhead, returned as rects so the draw and the hit-test consume the same list. The derivation exists in cutaway.galleon_rooms, tangled up with hull tapers and a 3D floor; the 2D plan wants the weights and none of the hull. This is what the whole lower half of the screen stands on, and it must return rects rather than draw, exactly as "split composition from polling" demands.
  • A compartment surface that is not ui_card. ui_card is parchment plus a frame plus (at Floating) a shadow. A berth needs three grounds on one axis: paper (fitted), bare timber (empty), shaded paper (unavailable). Ui_Emphasis covers the third; there is no vocabulary at all for "this surface is hull, not paper", and inventing it at the call site is how a screen ends up reaching for a colour. Wanted: a Ui_Ground the widget takes, with ui_ink already knowing which text tones each ground admits — which also settles what muted ink means over sand, a pairing the guide's two-grounds rule does not currently cover.
  • Text that measures and elides. ui_text places a measured string in a rect; nothing shortens one that does not fit. A 128px Medium berth cannot hold "Captain's Quarters", and the plan is not shippable until ui_text_fit(rect, text, ...) -> (drawn: string, fit: bool) exists — with a truncation mark that is not U+2026, which the atlas does not carry.
  • A ledger that hands back its cells. draw_build_ledger measures its own columns and draws them, and arms the whole strip as the burn target. Here one cell of it is the drop target and the rest stays a readout, so the measuring has to be split out: build_ledger_cells(rect, fields) -> []rl.Rectangle, with the draw and the hit-test both reading the result.
  • A leader between two rects. The rule tying her mark on the chart to her plan below is one DrawRectangleRec; the part worth owning is the derivation — which edge, struck where, stopping at what. ui_leader(from, to) -> rl.Rectangle. Wrong once by hand, wrong again the moment either end moves.
  • Not a ui.odin item, but recorded so it is not rediscovered: the style guide's recessive faded-ink tone is missing from harness.css's :root and from check-mock.py's ROSTER, and it is not a shade of any swatch that is there. Every recessive mark on this mockup — the fog nodes, the unsailed routes, the reading card's divider — borrows --sand instead, which is a touch too warm and too present for the job. Adding the swatch is a one-line change to two files that this mockup was not allowed to make.
