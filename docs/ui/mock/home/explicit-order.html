Order: the content is ordered by an explicit reading order rather than by where a thing happens to sit in the world.

The constraint: the content is ordered by an explicit reading order, not by where a thing sits on the hull. So the screen's index is a register — eight rows in a fixed sequence (four Exposed, then four Concealed), each row cut into seven columns: berth · size · cover · fitting · effect · tags · weight · cargo. The galleon is still on screen, blocked in at the left, but she is the subject of the register rather than its index. Nothing on this screen requires you to point at anything.

What the constraint forced

The hover card had to be dissolved into columns. Today draw_ship_slot_card writes one berth up on parchment and ties it back to a room with a leader line, and its last line is a run-on: wt 18 · 0/0 · top deck · Exposed. A register cannot keep that string — a run-on detail line is exactly the thing a table exists to take apart. So the four facts became four cells, and every one of them is now comparable down a column: you can see at a glance that the gun deck's Long Nines is the heaviest thing aboard and that hold 3 is the one running two-thirds empty. That comparison did not exist on the shipped screen at any cost, because only one berth's card can be up at a time.

The size rule stopped needing to be explained. While something is in hand, the shipped surface lights the berths it may land in and dims the rest. A dim over six of eight rows is exactly what this constraint forbids, so drag state was moved out of the seven data columns entirely and into a 22px mark column that exists for nothing else: a caret on the berth that can take it, a socket on the berth it came out of, and nothing anywhere else. The reason the other six are unavailable needs no treatment at all — it is already printed in the size · cover column, one cell away. A hull cannot say “Large”; a table already did.

Hover became the only thing it is allowed to be. The cursor is on top crew. Its ground lifts one shade (#fbe8b2, parchment × 1.068) and it takes the caret — and that is all, because the row already said everything it has to say before the mouse arrived. The guide's hover rule (“the caret moves, the ground lifts”) turns out to port onto parchment unchanged, which was not obvious going in.

The card in hand became a row. Drag-and-drop over a hull needs a ghost that follows the cursor, because the target is a place. Over a list the target is a row, and a ghost sliding over rows obscures the rows it is asking you to choose between. So the fitting in hand is drawn as a row of the same table, in the same columns, raised off the surface with a cast shadow (ADR-0032 — a card in hand is one that has been lifted off, never one faded on the spot), parked at the head of the register where the reading order starts. That makes rearranging a two-step lift-and-place rather than a press-drag-release. It is a real change to the gesture and it should be argued on its merits: a drag across a vertical list is a click with a wrist movement attached.

The two destructive acts are told apart by extent, not by a second colour. Both are ui_alarm — the roster has exactly one danger tone and it is not negotiable, and BUILD_DANGER already reasons about the two being up together while a laden fitting is in the air. What the register adds is that a target can be the width of what it destroys: Over the Side spans the whole row because it takes the whole fitting, and burn 24 spans only the weight and cargo columns because the crates are all it takes. The cost is honest and worth stating: the burn target is a 118×34 click box where the shipped ledger gives it 1164×34. Both still open build_confirm_yes_rect's gate before anything is destroyed — that frame is not drawn here.

The stats became the table's foot. Hull, SPD, Cargo and Weight are draw_build_ledger's four measured cells kept verbatim, but they now sit under the columns they total: the weight column adds to 122 and the cargo column to 50/90. A strip along the bottom of the window was a readout beside the ship; the same four terms at the foot of a register are the last line of a sum you can check.

What it cost. The world is nearly gone. ADR-0032 says this screen's body is the world, and a 920×604 sheet of parchment leaves it a 268px margin — the galleon is a thumbnail, the sun and the islands do not fit, and the pleasure of the shipped 02-home.png is mostly not here. That is the trade the constraint demands and it should not be softened: a screen that can be read at rest is not the same screen as one that can be looked at. (No vignette, per the same ADR.)

What I would need from presentation/ui.odin that does not exist yet

  • A column model — ui_columns(rect, widths: []f32) -> []rl.Rectangle, and a Ui_Table that holds one so a header, eight rows, a totals row and two act targets all measure off the same array. This is the component the whole design stands on. Everything on this screen is x0 + i*pitch today, and there is no vocabulary at all for “the fourth cell of this band”. Written by hand it is eight literals per row, wrong the first time a column moves.
  • A row band with a ground axis — ui_row(rect, Ui_Row_Ground) over enum { Plain, Stripe, Lifted, Source }, resolving to one colour_shade factor or one roster swatch. The zebra, the hover lift and the source mark are three coordinated fills today and a screen could ship having got two of three; one axis is what stops that, the same way Ui_Emphasis.Unavailable does for dimming.
  • A ground above parchment. Ui_Emphasis only goes down (Primary → Unavailable), and UI_SURFACE_TINT tops out at 1.0. Hover on a parchment row needs a shade brighter than the panel it sits on (colour_shade(COLOUR_PARCHMENT, 1.068)), which nothing in the widget layer can currently ask for. Either the tint table gains a value above 1 or there is a Ui_Elevation.Lifted that means it.
  • Right-anchored numeric cells. ui_text now takes Ui_Anchor, which is most of it — but a numeric column wants digit alignment (6/25 under 24/40), and Pixel Operator is proportional. Either a ui_text_numeric that pads to the widest value in the column, or the honest answer that a register wants the mono cut of the face and that is a second atlas.
  • A held-fitting state that is not a cursor ghost. Build_Drag is press-drag-release: build_drop_command is only ever called from IsMouseButtonReleased, and draw_build_ghost centres a card on the mouse. Lift-and-place needs Build_Held — a fitting and its origin slot that survive between two separate clicks — plus a build_place_command that takes a target row instead of a release point. The mapping from source + target to Command_Refit is unchanged; only what ends the gesture is different, which is why this is a small change wearing a large hat.
  • ui_alarm on a sub-rect of the thing it is arming. Today it washes and borders a whole rect, so a target is always the whole surface. “This act destroys these two columns” needs the alarm to take a rect derived from the column model above — which is fine, but it means the hit-test and the wash must come from one derivation or the coral will end up sitting somewhere the click does not.
  • The bands have to be returned, not drawn. The loop hit-tests eight berth rows, two act rects and a chart tab that it also draws. With a table this regular the two cannot be written twice: whatever computes the register returns the rects, and both the draw and the poll consume them (the guide's “split composition from polling”, applied to layout). build_slot_at currently asks cutaway.galleon_room_at — a register needs the equivalent, and it is a division rather than a hit-test against geometry.

What I did not need: a third type size. The whole screen is 32/16 with colour, ground and column position carrying every rank on it.
