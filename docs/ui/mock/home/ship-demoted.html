Figure/ground: the galleon is demoted to a 320x87 legend in the corner, so she is a label on the screen rather than its subject.

The galleon is 320×87 in the top-right corner and she is a legend, not a picture. Two masts, a deck line, a keel, and her eight berths as numbered pips in her own geography: 1–4 above the waterline (exposed), 5–8 below it (concealed). The screen's horizon rule is her waterline, so she floats on the same line the world does. Everything she says, the ledger says at reading size; her one remaining job is to answer "where is berth 6, actually" — which is exactly the job the full-scale hull was doing for free, and the only one worth keeping.

What the constraint forced

The berths had to leave the hull, so the split had to be re-carried. Today geography is the whole mechanism (ADR-0030): a fitting is exposed because it is drawn on the weather deck and concealed because it is drawn in the belly, and no badge says so. Demote the hull and that mechanism goes with it. It comes back as two headed columns — Exposed / Concealed — with the visibility repeated per row, and the pips keyed 1–8 so the two readings can be checked against each other. The diagram is subordinate to the ledger, which is the inversion: today the numbers refer to the picture, here the picture refers to the numbers.

Inspect stopped being a callout and became a dock. The shipped inspect is a hover card tethered to a room by a leader line — it exists only while the cursor is on the hull, and it vanishes the moment you look away, which is the definition of chrome rather than matter. With no hull to tether to, and with "readable matter" as the brief, it became a selected berth with a permanent 352px rail: the name at Title, the effect, the tags, the detail line, and the three verbs that berth admits. You can read it while you drag something else, which the tethered version cannot do.

The numbers grew two levels. Hull / SPD / Cargo / Weight are 16px terms in a 34px strip pinned under the hull today (BUILD_LEDGER_Y 650, BUILD_LEDGER_H 34) — thin chrome laid over the subject. Here they are .Display on a 1196×116 slab directly under the heading, and they are deliberately larger than the screen's own name. Hull and Cargo are ratios and get a bar; SPD and Weight are scalars and do not. That asymmetry is the point of a bar: it makes the denominator readable without arithmetic.

Burning could not stay on the ledger. The shipped burn is a drop onto the bottom stats strip — good reasoning while the strip is quiet chrome (draw_build_ledger: the ledger is both what the burn costs and where it is paid). Promote the stats to the loudest object on the screen and that target becomes a drop onto the headline. So burning got its own card beside the bin, and the two are told apart by how much coral each spends: Over the Side washes its whole card, because the whole fitting goes; Strike the Cargo colours a bar exactly the width of the load, because only the load goes and the hold stays fitted. Coral appearing twice is BUILD_DANGER's own carve-out — one signal on the only two places it is true — and both are on screen only because a laden berth is in the air in this frame.

An empty berth is a hole in the paper. Filled berths are cards; berth 2 (bare) and berth 5 (in hand) are outlines with the sea showing through, and their pips match. That is the read the shipped screen cannot give at a glance, because an empty room on the cutaway is just a slightly emptier piece of ship.

What I would need that does not exist

.Display is not on this list: ui_font_display is already baked (ui_fonts_load) and ui_font_for(.Display) already resolves, so the 48px values port through ui_text unchanged. Only the harness lacked the size, and this file declares --display: 48px locally, as the mock README asks.

  • ui_meter(rect, value, of, ground) — a ratio bar. Nothing in ui_widgets.odin draws one, and no_screen_strokes_its_own_chrome forbids a screen reaching for the two rectangles itself. Wanted three times on this screen alone (Hull, Cargo, and the strike target's load).
  • A surface with no ground. Every Ui_Frame carries parchment, so there is no way to draw an empty berth as a framed hole over the water. Either a fourth frame or ui_outline(rect, weight, tone).
  • A "lit" direction on Ui_Emphasis. The axis only runs Primary → Unavailable, i.e. one way, into the ground. A legal drop target has to come forward. ui_alarm is the only widget that does, and it is coral by law and therefore unusable for a target that is not destructive. Wanted: ui_target(rect, live), its non-destructive twin, painting a roster ground.
  • ui_stat(rect, label, value, level) — label muted above, value at level below, measured. draw_build_ledger packs this by hand today with its own measure-and-gutter loop; a four-column Display version wants the same logic again, and that is the second copy.
  • A per-berth index that is presentation's to name. The pips key to the rows by ship.Slot_Index, which exists — but nothing in the presentation package agrees on rendering it, and a diagram-plus-ledger design lives or dies on the two agreeing.

Everything else ports off the README's table: absolute rects, roster fills, one-axis gradients, frame-panel/frame-card/frame-button as ui_panel/ui_card/ui_button, the carets as rl.DrawTriangle, and the ghost's shadow as the second rectangle ui_card(…, .Floating) already draws.
