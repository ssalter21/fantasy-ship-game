At Anchor — the paper is the ground

Ground: the paper is the ground. Sea and sky are a 32px rim around the sheet and one aperture cut through it.

The constraint: this screen is mostly parchment. The sea and sky are a 32px rim around the sheet, a 4px sliver under it, and one aperture cut through the paper at lower right. Today the world fills the frame edge to edge and the chrome is laid over it; here a 1180×612 sheet is the frame and the world is what shows around and through it.

What the constraint forced, and none of it was "a bigger panel"

  • The galleon had to stop being the thing you inspect. The shipped screen is a lit three-quarter cutaway and her berths are her rooms — that only works when she is the field. Demote her to a margin and you cannot pick a room off her. So the berths became a sheer draught ruled on the paper, and the one thing that made the move survivable is that a draught has a waterline too: exposed above it, concealed below, a berth's length still her size. Geography carries the split (ADR-0030) in ink exactly as it carried it in geometry.
  • Containment had to be carved, not stacked. Parchment laid on parchment is invisible. A filled berth is a well pressed into the sheet — face shaded 0.940, dark rule leading, light rule trailing — and an empty berth is a hairline outline on bare paper. Filled and empty differ in material, not in how much writing they carry, which is what makes eight berths legible at a glance with no badge on any of them. Below the waterline the wells are cut deeper (0.898) into an already-washed belly (0.953), so the concealed rank is a darker place on the paper as well as in the ship.
  • The card in hand could not be a ui_card. Its face is parchment inside a 2px --sea-deep border — which over water is a card, and on a parchment field is indistinguishable from the one thing the roster lets me mark a legal berth with. So the two had to split: sea-deep stays the "act here" border and goes to the legal berth, and the fitting in hand became raised paper — bare parchment, a cliff edge, a cast shadow. It falls out nicely: every printed thing on this screen is a shade darker than the sheet because every one is cut into it, so the one thing that is the sheet's own full brightness is the one thing lifted off it. It also had to be moved to straddle the waterline rule, because a floating card sitting level with a rank of berths reads as a ninth berth.
  • The legal berth is promoted; the illegal ones are not demoted. The shipped drag lights the legal berths and dims the rest. Dimming seven of eight cells on an all-parchment field is ruinous — there is no bright ground under the paper to borrow contrast from, so a demoted cell just goes quiet and the page goes with it. Only top crew is marked, with a 2px --sea-deep rule: the roster's own "interactive border on parchment", already spoken for, and not a new signal.
  • Overboard became the aperture, and that is an improvement the constraint paid for. The shipped bin is a coral box at bottom left, and it reads because the whole screen is sea and a box is the odd object on it. Invert the grounds and a coral box on paper is just another box. But there is exactly one piece of real water on this screen — so the drop target is the water. Nothing has to be explained.
  • The two destructive acts stopped needing their labels to be told apart. Shipped, they are two coral rectangles distinguished by wording. Here one is water and the other is a line in the books: different material, different half of the screen, different consequence. Coral is up twice, and for the same reason BUILD_DANGER already argues — one signal on the only two places it is true, neither of them on screen unless something is already in hand.
  • The stats stopped hiding. draw_build_ledger replaces all four figures with the burn instruction when it arms, which is the one moment the brief's "always visible" is broken. A ledger row has room under it, so the instruction sits beneath the figure it would change and Cargo 50/90 stays up while you decide.
  • The hover card became a printed slot. A description card that follows the cursor is right over a world and wrong over a page — it occludes the very thing it describes. So the sheet carries one printed slot for a berth's particulars, keyed to the plan by a leader run out into the margin, and it holds still while your hands are busy: it can describe the Captain's Quarters while a hold is in the air, which the shipped surface cannot do at all.
  • Two of the four stats turned out to be ratios. On a page, "100/100" wants a ruled gauge under it and "SPD 4" wants a plain hairline — so the rule under a row now says what kind of number the row is carrying. That distinction did not exist when the four sat in one sentence along the bottom of the sea.
  • The chart stopped being a tab over water and became the sheet underneath. chart_offset already slides it up a full window height from below; when the ground is paper, the thing that rises from below is another sheet. Its foot shows along the bottom edge with the caret and the words, and raising it is one sheet sliding over another. No re-timing, no new gesture.
  • .over-water is spent exactly once, on Over the side inside the aperture. Every other word on the screen is ink on paper. The inverse of the shipped screen, where the heading, the tab and the whole stat line are cream over the sea.
  • No vignette, and she is blocked in. The framing signal on a paper screen is the paper's edge (style guide, Framing); a 150px dark ramp would eat the entire margin. And the galleon in the aperture is five rectangles — hull, two masts, a rail, a wake — because she already exists and already looks right, and a worse CSS copy of a solved thing would cost the half of the screen that is the actual question. What the constraint changes is how much of her you get: her hull and two mast feet, cropped by the cut.

What I would need from presentation/ui.odin that does not exist yet

The widget set has moved a long way — ui_card, ui_alarm, ui_divider, Ui_Emphasis.Unavailable and the named spacing scale all landed, and this mockup leans on every one of them. Six things it had to fake with bare rectangles, each a component with an axis rather than a one-off:

  • Ui_Elevation.Inset is a uniform darkening, and a well is a bevel. ui_surface tints the whole nine-slice by 0.92, which reads as dimmed — the same thing Ui_Emphasis.Muted says. Twelve surfaces on this page are wells, and every one of them needs a dark leading edge and a light trailing edge: that is what says "pressed in" rather than "turned down". One tint cannot produce it. This is the single component the design lives or dies on, and it is the one the earlier Shop mockup asked for and got the name of but not the shape.
  • A second axis on ui_alarm: which destructive act. It takes (rect, lit) and paints one coral wash, so the two destructive targets are identical objects separated only by their words. This screen needs them to differ before you read anything — ui_alarm(rect, lit, .Overboard) washing a world rect, .Strike ruling a ledger row and leaving its figure legible under the wash. Coral is legal in exactly one proc, so the distinction has nowhere else it can live.
  • A gauge: ui_meter(rect, filled, total, ground). A track and a fill, both roster tones, whole pixels, one axis. Nothing draws one today — ui_divider is a line at a weight with no fraction in it. Two of the four stats are ratios and every screen that shows Hull or Cargo wants this.
  • An aperture: chrome with a hole in it. ui_panel and ui_card only ever lay paper on the world. This design needs the inverse — the world showing through a cut, with the cut's own sill — and there is no widget that composes world-inside-chrome in either direction. Faked here by painting the water back inside the rect, which is a lie the moment anything behind the sheet moves.
  • A deckled sheet edge. The guide's own framing rule is "the torn parchment edge, not a dark vignette", and UI_FRAME_PANEL's border runs are straight — fine for a panel inside a screen, wrong when the sheet is the screen, where a ruled rectangle reads as a dialog box. A 9-slice cannot carry it (the edge has to vary along its run), so this is an edge-strip blit: a new draw capability, not a new constant, and worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering.
  • A leader, and a vertical anchor. An annotated draught is held together by leaders — ui_leader(from, to), whose elbow routing (which side, how far into the margin) is exactly what should not be typed at a call site. And Ui_Anchor is horizontal only: ui_text always centres vertically in its rect, so every one of the ~30 strings on this page is placed by handing it a 16px rect at a hand-computed y. A vertical anchor, or a ui_row(rect, index), is what turns a ruled page into a rhythm instead of thirty numbers.

Two things I did not need. No third type size — a 32px heading and 16px everywhere else carries a sheet this large, with colour ranking within each block, and the constraint never pushed against it. And no new swatch: three literals appear here and all three are colour_shade of parchment — 0.953 the belly wash, 0.940 a well on bare sheet, 0.898 a well below the waterline and a gauge track. Three factors are the whole of this screen's depth, which is the argument for colour_shade rather than for three more names on the roster.

Both destructive targets are drawn at UI_ALARM_WASH, not UI_ALARM_LIT, because the cursor is up by the hold with the fitting in hand and is over neither of them. Only the one under the cursor may light — that difference is the entire "a release here is the one that acts" signal, and a mockup that spends it on both at once has thrown it away to look busier.
