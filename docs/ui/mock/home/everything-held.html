Containment: every piece of content sits inside a bounded, visibly framed container. Nothing bleeds off an edge and nothing floats unbacked over the water.

The constraint: every piece of content sits inside a bounded, visibly framed container. Nothing bleeds off an edge; nothing floats unbacked over the water. The screen is six held regions with a gutter of sea between them.

What the constraint forced

The stats stopped being a strip. Today they are a full-bleed bar welded to y = 700 — an element with three of its edges off-screen, which is the opposite of held. Here they are four wells sunk into a board that also carries the screen's name, and the board stops 16px short on all four sides. Each stat has a rectangle of its own, so "Cargo 50/90" is a thing in a box rather than a phrase in a run of text. The board being one plate is what buys it: a title and four numbers do not each deserve their own panel, and they do not need one when a single region can hold them both.

The inspect card lost its leader line. The line exists because the card floats — an unanchored card over open water has no way to say which berth it is about except by drawing a wire to it. Held in a column, it says so in words (Berth · top deck) and the berth answers with a caret and a heavier frame. Two signals instead of one wire, and the screen loses its only diagonal. The detail line is unchanged — wt 18 · 0/0 · top deck · Exposed — because holding a card changes where it sits, not what it says.

The ship became a window instead of the screen. This is the move the whole exercise turns on. She is drawn into an 848×284 framed viewport with sky for a ground and the sea at its foot; her belly is a second, dimmer window below it, and the waterline is the divider between the two frames rather than a line drawn on the world. Exposed and Concealed are then carried by which container a berth sits in — ADR-0030's geography, kept, but geography made out of frames. She reads smaller than she does today, and that cost is not hidden: a galleon in a box is a model of a galleon, and the screen gives up some of the pleasure of standing in front of the real one.

A card in hand is the one thing "hold everything" cannot hold, so it was given a container: the In Hand region. The gesture still begins as a lift from a berth — hold 1 is drawn as the socket it became — but the fitting's home while lifted is a named slot rather than the cursor, and every berth that will take it wears a fits chip. The gain is that the drop is legible with the mouse nowhere near it, which a cursor-borne ghost never is; the price is that press-drag-release now has a second reading (lift, then place) that build_surface_loop would have to accept alongside it.

Empty had to become a socket, not a faint card. A held layout must answer "is anything in this box" without alpha, because a translucent card over a bright ground reads as a stain (ADR-0032). So a filled berth is paper and an empty one is a hole cut in the deck — timber ground, cliff border, cream over it, the ink rule reversed because the ground is. top crew is empty and hold 1 is vacated, and the two are the same object saying different words.

The two destructive acts are shaped differently, not merely worded differently. Over the Side is outlined in coral all the way round: the whole object goes. Burn the Cargo keeps an inert cliff frame and takes coral only on its edge and its crate: the berth stays, its contents do not. Coral therefore appears twice, which BUILD_DANGER in presentation/build_surface.odin already reasons about — one signal, on the only two places it is true, and neither on screen unless something is in hand.

What it cost: the water. Hold everything and the sea becomes a 16px gutter, one strip at the foot of the deck window, and a horizon glimpsed between the columns. The style guide says a screen whose body is the world draws no vignette; this screen's body is no longer the world. There is still no vignette here — but now for the opposite reason.

The version stamp had to be given a home. It is the one mark on the shipped screen that belongs to no region — it hangs in the top-right corner over open water, which under this rule is exactly what nothing is allowed to do. It sits at the foot of the ship's panel here. That is the constraint at its most pedantic, and it is worth showing rather than quietly exempting: a rule that holds everything holds the version stamp too, or it is a rule about the interesting things only.

No third type size: 32px for the screen's name (which is what draw_build_heading already draws at .Title), 16px for every other string. Three literal hexes, each a uniform shade one colour_shade call reproduces: #dfce9d (parchment 0.95 — the inset wells and the dimmed berths), #4c3723 (rock 0.603 — the belly's timber), #afa68d (cream-bright 0.72 — the muted tone on that timber).

What this needs from presentation/ui.odin that does not exist

1. A viewport — the only thing here with no draw call behind it. ui_panel gives a bounded surface; nothing gives a bounded world. draw_ship_cutaway and the backdrop draw at WINDOW_WIDTH/WINDOW_HEIGHT, and cutaway.View is built at the logical frame size precisely so picking stays in the mouse's coordinate system. To put the galleon in a frame I need something like ui_viewport(rect) -> Ship_Framing: a scissor push/pop around the draw (rl.BeginScissorMode) and a View built at the rect's origin and size, so galleon_room_at still resolves against the rooms the eye is looking at (issue 476). Without the second half the frame is decoration and every click is off by the frame's offset.

2. A ground that is neither parchment nor water. ui_ink knows .Parchment and .Water. An empty berth's ground is timber, and its secondary row had to be hand-written here as colour_shade(COLOUR_CREAM_BRIGHT, 0.72) — exactly the hand-picked tone Ui_Emphasis exists to abolish. Want a Ui_Ground.Timber with the whole Primary→Unavailable ramp defined against it.

3. A recessed frame. Ui_Elevation is Inset / Flush / Raised / Floating, and all four describe something sitting on a surface. An empty berth has to read as a hole in one, and so do the stat wells. That is a UI_FRAME_SOCKET 9-slice in presentation/ui_frame.odin — one whose light runs the other way round — plus an elevation that reaches it.

4. A chip. fits is one word in its own bounded box in a card's corner. ui_button is a control and ui_card is a surface; a chip is neither, and without one every screen that wants it draws a raw rectangle and trips no_screen_strokes_its_own_chrome.

5. A caret with a direction. ui_icon's caret points right, because it is a menu row's margin marker. draw_home_chart_caret already had to hand-wind a rotated one; this mockup needs a second, pointing down at a berth. Two hand-wound carets is the point at which the icon should take a direction instead of each call site re-deriving the winding raylib's cull demands.

6. Sub-rect arithmetic — not a layout system. The guide declines a layout system deliberately and this is not a request for one. But holding everything turned one screen into roughly twenty nested rectangles, and ui_inset(rect, Ui_Space), ui_rows(rect, n, gap) and ui_cols(rect, widths, gap) returning rects would let a Home_Layout hold four numbers instead of forty — which is what keeps the screen steerable under --workbench rather than a rebuild per nudge.
