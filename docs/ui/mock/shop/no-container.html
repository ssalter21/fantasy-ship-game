Shop — no container

The constraint: there is no panel and no card. Nothing on this screen has a container — no parchment ground behind any text, no border around any group, no rectangle whose job is to hold something else. The sea shows through everywhere between the marks.

What the constraint forced

1. Legibility moved from the ground into the glyph. The shipped screen solves this by putting solid --parchment under every word and darkening the water beneath it with a cast shadow. With the parchment gone, two things carry the type and neither of them is a rectangle. The first is hue: the warm neutrals are the one family a saturated cyan cannot absorb, so the hierarchy runs --cream-bright → --parchment → --sand → --cliff, four warm steps all clashing with the same cool ground — the style guide's own contrast engine, used as a text ramp rather than as a world palette. The second is an edge: every run is drawn three times — --ink at (-1, -1), --ink at (+1, +1), then its own tone on top. That is three DrawTextEx calls, and it is the same bargain the harness already strikes for box-shadow — refuse the free effect (text-shadow is banned), allow the honest extra draw. One corner was measured and was not enough: a cream glyph sitting on a foam whitecap disappeared into it, and only bracketing the shape on both diagonals brought it back. Ink is never the colour of the text here; it is the colour of its edge. The fill stays warm-over-water, which is the rule.

2. The mockup had to make the sea genuinely hostile, or it proves nothing. Three whitecaps are placed to run through text — y 456 through "Small · Fire · Artifact", y 524 through "Leave", y 664 through "Chart". Foam is the brightest thing the roster allows, so cream-on-foam is this screen's worst case and it is the case to look at first. It survives because the ink edge is doing the work: on foam the glyph reads as a dark outline, on open sea as a light letter with a dark edge. A flat cyan ground would have let me dodge it.

The same test is built into the column's placement: the first two entries sit on sky, the third straddles the horizon at y 300, and the fourth sits on sea. A card would have made that irrelevant — it brings its own ground. Here one run of type has to hold on a mid-blue sky, on a saturated cyan, and across the hard line between them, which is the actual thing a container-less Shop has to survive.

3. Structure became rules, and the grammar is exactly two weights. A rule separates; it does not contain. There is not one vertical and not one corner anywhere on the screen, so nothing can close into a box. 1px is a separator between stock entries. 2px is a control — and the rule's length states the control's hit extent, which is information a bare label cannot carry once its button face is gone. "Leave" and "Chart" are the same object, mirrored: the rule sits above "Chart" only because the screen edge is below it.

4. Unaffordable had to be said without a sheet to shade. The shipped card states it with colour_shade(PARCHMENT, 0.95), which needs a card. "Titan's Heart" instead drops the whole entry two steps down the warm ramp (name to --sand, everything else to --cliff), its separator drops out of --foam into --sea-deep so it sinks toward the water, and its crate mark keeps the outline but loses its filled core. Still tone, never alpha: alpha over a bright sea does not dim anything, it just lets the world in.

5. The column got wider and the pitch got longer. Freed of a card edge, a line can come within 32px of the hull without colliding with anything, so the measure grew from the shipped 392px to 464px and the pitch from 104 to 104-with-32px-of-air below each rule. Air is the only thing left doing what padding used to do.

What I did not do. The vignette is present and unchanged — it composes under the chrome exactly as draw_offer_shop orders it, it is inset equally on all four edges, and it is not shaped to the content. Widening it, or sliding a band of darker sea under the column, would have been a panel with a soft edge, and that is the one move this brief exists to forbid.

What I would need from ui.odin that does not exist yet

presentation/ui.odin today is a colour roster, colour_shade, and two baked fonts. ui_frame.odin adds ui_nine_slice, ui_pixel_rect, ui_frame_min. Every widget it has assumes a container, which is precisely why this screen has nothing to build on. Five components, each with the axes it needs:

  • A text style that carries its own legibility over water. ui_text_over_water(font, text, pos, tone) — the two-draw edge above, so no call site re-decides the offset or the edge tone. Axes: tone (the fill), edge (off / one corner / two corners / full 8-way outline), edge_tone, and offset in whole pixels. edge_tone is a real axis and not a constant: an ink edge does nothing at all along the bottom of this screen, where the vignette has already taken the water down to near-ink, and that band wants a foam edge instead. The 8-way case is 9 DrawTextEx calls and should exist as a named cost rather than as something someone writes out by hand at 2am; if it turns out to be the common case, it is an argument for baking an outlined atlas instead, and that is a decision this component would make visible.
  • A text ramp for the water ground. Parchment has a real hierarchy — COLOUR_INK_PRIMARY / COLOUR_INK_MUTED / faded ink. Water has exactly one tone, COLOUR_CREAM_BRIGHT, and its comment says "never on parchment" without saying what the level below it is. This screen needed four levels over water and had to borrow PARCHMENT, SAND and CLIFF — swatches the roster names as surfaces, not as ink. That borrowing works (they are warm against a cool ground, which is the guide's own contrast engine), but it should be a named ramp — COLOUR_SEA_INK_PRIMARY … _SECONDARY … _MUTED … _DIM — not four call sites each deciding that sand is a text colour. Axis: one, rank, four stops.
  • A rule / divider with weight. ui_rule(rect, weight, tone), drawn with its own ink edge so a 1px hair survives a whitecap. Axes: weight (1px separator vs 2px control), tone, edge on/off, and extent — because the length is load-bearing here rather than decorative. Without a button face this is the only thing that says how wide a control is.
  • A control that is not a frame. Everything clickable in this game today goes through ui_nine_slice(UI_FRAME_BUTTON, …), which is a face. A ui_control_bare(rect, label, state) would take the same Ui_Button_State and express idle / hover / press with the caret and a rule weight instead of a fill. Axes: state, rule_edge (which side the rule sits on — "Chart" needs it above because it is on the screen edge), align. Hover is the open question this mockup does not answer: the style guide says hover lifts a translucent ground, and there is no ground here to lift, so hover has to fall entirely to the caret plus a rule-weight change — worth settling deliberately rather than discovering.
  • A measure-and-place text helper, finally. The style guide's "what this guide does not cover" already names this as the one missing helper, and a container-less layout is where its absence actually bites: the price rail is right-aligned to x 1212 and "Chart" is centred on x 622, and with no box to align inside, both are MeasureTextEx at the call site or they are wrong. Axes: anchor (left / centre / right), against a rect or against a single coordinate.

One thing this screen does not need, and it is worth recording: no new draw capability. Every mark above is a DrawRectangleRec, a DrawRectangleGradientV, or a DrawTextEx — the port table in docs/ui/mock/README.md covers all of it. What is missing is not the renderer. It is that the widget layer only knows how to draw onto paper, and this screen has no paper.
