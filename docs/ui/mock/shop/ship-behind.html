Shop — the ship is the ground, the stock lies on her

The constraint. She is the background and she fills the frame: her side runs edge to edge from the rail at y 236 to the waterline at y 604, which is 53% of the window's height, and the only clear water left is the strip her bow-wave stands in. The four cards are laid on her, and each one is pinned beside the berth its fitting would occupy — the two Fire weapons beside gunports (the forward chase and the waist battery), the small Fire artifact beside the quarter gun, and the Brace artifact beside the hold's loading port amidships. A leader line and a ring say which. Nothing sits in a reserved strip; the reading runs bow to stern and the way out is past her stern in open water.

What that forced, against the shipped screen.

  • OFFER_SHOP_LAYOUT is gone in its entirety. There is no col_x, no col_w, no pitch: a card's rect is derived from an anchor on the ship, not from an index times a pitch. That is the single largest cost of the constraint and the source of most of the list below.
  • Card width fell 392 → 300. Four cards have to coexist across one frame instead of stacking down one edge. 300 is close to the floor: the longest intent string, "+7 Offense when its condition holds", measures ~256px at 16px, and the card's inner width at 300 is 268.
  • The cards now sit on planking, not on water. That is better for contrast — dark trunk under parchment beats bright sea under parchment — but it makes the "dim by tone, never by alpha" rule (ADR-0032) load-bearing rather than merely correct: an alpha-dimmed unaffordable card here would have her strakes reading straight through it. And the shipped shade factor is no longer strong enough: 0.95 is measured against bright water, and against her shadowed underbody it is invisible — the unaffordable card came back as the brightest thing on screen. It takes parchment shaded 0.88, an inert cliff border instead of the interactive sea-deep one, muted ink — and its leader and ring go to cliff too, so the quieting reaches all the way to the part of the ship it points at. The general rule that falls out: a dim factor is relative to the ground the card is on, and once cards are scattered over a ship the ground is no longer one constant.
  • Leave came off the bottom of the column and went to the water astern. It is no longer "the last row"; it is where the reading ends.
  • Draw order is now load-bearing in two new places: the cast shadows have to land over her planking rather than under it, and every leader must be drawn before its card so a card covers its own leader's root and never the feature at the leader's far end.
  • The stat line moved to the top left, because the reading now starts at the bow.

What this needs from ui.odin that does not exist. Six things, and the first two are the ones without which this design is not portable at all.

  • A screen-space anchor on the ship. ship_feature_point(ship, feature) -> rl.Vector2. She is lit 3D geometry drawn through a camera; the Shop's chrome today knows nothing about where any part of her lands in the logical frame. Axes: the feature (.Gunport_Forward, .Gunport_Waist, .Gunport_Quarter, .Hold_Port, .Rail…), and the camera. Every number in this mockup is hand-placed against a CSS hull, which is exactly the thing that cannot survive the port.
  • A leader line from a card edge to a point. ui_leader(card: rl.Rectangle, edge: Edge, to: rl.Vector2, thickness: f32, tone: rl.Color). Axes: which edge it leaves (so a card left of its anchor leaves right, one above leaves down), the anchor, thickness, tone. Today it would be a bare DrawRectangleRec per segment with the elbow computed at the call site — i.e. re-decided four times.
  • An anchor ring. ui_anchor_ring(rect, thickness, tone) — a hollow rectangle laid on a world feature. DrawRectangleLinesEx already draws it; what is missing is that it is a named chrome mark with a live and an inert tone, so "unaffordable" can reach it without a fourth call site re-deriving the rule.
  • A placement solver. ui_place_beside(anchor: rl.Vector2, size: rl.Vector2, prefer: []Side, keep_out: []rl.Rectangle, bounds: rl.Rectangle) -> (rl.Rectangle, Side). With the pitch gone, something has to choose a card's rect: beside its anchor, inside the frame, not over another card, and not over any feature another card is pointing at. Axes: anchor, card size, preferred sides in order, the keep-out set, the stage. This is the piece that replaces OFFER_SHOP_LAYOUT, and it is genuinely new work — it is a solver, not a layout struct.
  • A card with a tail. The leader above is the cheap answer; the better one is UI_FRAME_CARD_TAIL — a card frame whose border carries a pointer. A 9-slice cannot express it (the tail is not on a slice boundary and moves), so this is a new sprite plus a DrawTriangle, with axes: which edge the tail leaves and how far along that edge. Worth naming even if the leader ships first, because the two are alternatives and picking one is a decision.
  • Hit-testing and traversal over a scattered rect set. ui_pick(rects: []rl.Rectangle, mouse) -> int plus an explicit traversal order. option_screen_boxes' idiom — a stack, so index order is reading order — does not survive scattering. The caret has to move bow-to-stern, which means the order is data now, not the array index.

Type. Nothing here proposes a third size; every string is 16px, and the hierarchy is carried by colour exactly as the style guide has it.

One note on the gate, not on the design. Every value rule check-mock.py expresses passes on this file — colours (nine literals, every one a colour_shade of trunk or parchment, which the checker names), one-axis gradients, whole pixels, the 1244×700 stage. It still exits 1, because the script assumes a mockup is a sibling of the harness in three places: it reads path.parent / "harness.css" (which throws for a mockup in a subdirectory), it greps for the literal href="harness.css" (this file correctly links ../harness.css), and its no-argument default globs docs/ui/mock/*.html non-recursively, so a subdirectory is never swept at all. Three one-line fixes, in a file this mockup was told not to touch — recorded here so the next author does not spend the afternoon looking for a colour that is off the roster.
