Order: the stock does not run down a column, so it is dealt around the galleon, one card against each side of her hull.

The constraint: the stock does not run down a column. It is dealt around the galleon — one card against each side of her hull rect (400,372 452×156), tethered to it by a 2px rule, and sized by price rank: Titan's Heart at 120 is a 372×148 card, Chain & Bar Shot at 34 is 344×120, and Outriggers and Long Nines at 26 and 18 share the 104px small size at two widths. There is no pitch anywhere in this layout, and no two cards are the same shape.

What that forced. The shipped screen orders the stock by position in a list, which is an order the data does not have — the four fittings arrive unranked and the column asserts one anyway. Taking the column away meant finding a real order, and price was the only one on the card already. So size became the price signal, which then does the unaffordable card's work for free: the dearest fitting is the largest object on the screen and simultaneously the dullest, which reads as "the big prize, and not for you" in a way a dimmed row three of four never could. It still dims by tone (#dfce9d, parchment shaded 0.95, plus a cliff border and muted ink), never by alpha — ADR-0032's rule holds whatever the layout is.

Placing paper on all four sides of the ship also changes who the ship is. In the column she is scenery with 424px of screen to herself; here she is the armature, and the cards have to clear her rigging and her waterline rather than merely stay off her. The tethers are what make that legible instead of accidental — each card sits where its fitting would go aboard: chain shot aloft at the masts, the brace amidships against her frames, the outriggers off her beam, the long nines under the gundeck. And Leave stops being "the last row of the column" the moment there is no column; it takes the one quarter of the ring the stock does not use.

The cost, stated honestly. Four cards at four corners is more eye travel than four rows in one place, and a fifth item has nowhere obvious to go — a ring has four sides. If the Shop ever stocks six, this layout is the wrong one and should be said so rather than stretched.

What I would need from ui.odin that does not exist yet

Every one of these exists today as literals inside draw_offer_shop_card, which knows exactly one geometry (392×92) and exactly one way to stack it.

  • A card at three sizes — ui_offer_card(rect, item, size) with Card_Size :: enum { Small, Medium, Large }. The axis is the size class and it drives padding and row pitch together (14/30, 16/36, 22/44); the width stays free, because width is set by the longest intent line and not by the size class. One proc that reflows its four rows from a rect, not three procs.
  • A rank-to-size map — card_size_for(price, stock). Size is only honest if it is derived from the stock the shop actually rolled; hand-assigning Large to Titan's Heart in a layout struct would make this mockup a lie the first time prices differ.
  • A placement helper that works against another rect, not against an origin — ui_place_beside(anchor, side, size) -> rl.Rectangle, centring a card on one of an anchor rect's four sides with a gap. Today all layout is x0/y0 + i*pitch; there is no vocabulary at all for "beside this other thing". This is the component the whole mockup stands on.
  • A tether — ui_tether(card, anchor) -> rl.Rectangle. The draw is one DrawRectangleRec; the part worth having is the derivation, and it is less obvious than it looks — the stub is struck at the midpoint of the span the two rects share, not at the card's own midpoint, which on three of these four points at open water beside her. That is the kind of thing that is wrong once by hand and wrong four times when a card moves.
  • A price cluster with real right alignment — ui_price(right_edge, top, price, tone). The crate is a 14px outlined square drawn as a primitive (the style guide's "glyphs are shapes" rule), and putting it plus a 1–3 digit number flush right needs the measure-then-place text helper the style guide already records as the one missing layout helper.
  • The placement pass has to return rects, not draw them. The poll loop hit-tests the same four cards it draws, and with placement this irregular the two cannot be written twice. Whatever computes the ring returns [4]rl.Rectangle that both the draw and the hit-test consume — the style guide's "split composition from polling", applied to layout rather than to buttons.
