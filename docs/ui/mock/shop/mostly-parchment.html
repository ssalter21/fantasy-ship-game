Shop — mostly parchment

The constraint: this screen is mostly parchment. The world — sea, sky, the galleon — is a margin, not the field. A 936×624 sheet takes about two thirds of the frame and the sea is what shows around it: a wide strip down the left where the galleon rides, and twenty pixels of it along the top and right.

What that forced, and it was not "a wider column". Widening the shipped column would have kept every relationship the screen already has. Making the stop a page broke four of them:

  • The cards had to stop being cards. A parchment card over the sea is legible because the sea is under it. On an all-parchment field there is nothing to separate from, so the same card is invisible. The entries are pressed into the sheet instead — a shaded well with a dark rule above and a lighter one below — and the page is held together by rules rather than by boxes.
  • The stack became a ruled table. Once four entries share one sheet, name / effect / price want to be columns that line up down the page against a heading row, not three lines stacked inside each card. That is a different reading motion: the shipped screen is read card by card, this one is read down the price column.
  • Nothing on this screen is text over water. The stat line was cream over the sea; it is now the bill's letterhead, printed against the stop's name, so it takes --ink-muted. .over-water goes unused for the first time. Text tone follows its ground, and the constraint changed the ground under every word on the screen.
  • Dimming got harder, not easier. Tone-not-alpha was already the rule over a bright sea. Here alpha is not merely wrong, it is inert: there is only more paper underneath, so a translucent row would disappear rather than dim. Titan's Heart is parchment shaded 0.86 against the other entries' 0.94, with muted ink and both of its rules dropped to --cliff — stale paper, not faint paper. The gap between the two shades has to be bigger than the shipped screen's 0.95, because a shade competing against a whole parchment field has less contrast to borrow.
  • The Chart tab became a tab. It was a translucent bar over the sea. There is no sea left along the bottom edge to lay a bar on, so it is an index tab growing out of the sheet with no top rule — the sheet's own edge is its top rule. Same for the vignette: omitted, because the framing signal here is the paper edge (style guide, Framing), and a 150px dark ramp would consume the entire margin.
  • The galleon is cropped, on purpose. She is blocked in with shop.html's own five rectangles — hull, two masts, two sails — scaled to the margin, and the sheet cuts across her waist so her forward sail runs on behind the paper. Not redrawn in CSS: she already exists and already looks right, and a worse copy of a solved thing would only have cost the half of the screen that is actually the question. If the world is a margin, she is in the margin, and what changes is how much of her you get rather than what she is.

What I would need from ui.odin that does not exist

Every one of these is something this mockup faked with a bare rectangle, and every one is a component with an axis rather than a one-off:

  • A panel with a surface axis: raised | flush | inset. The single component this design lives or dies on. ui_nine_slice(UI_FRAME_PANEL, …) only knows how to make a thing sit above its ground. An inset variant — shaded face, dark rule leading, light rule trailing — is what turns a card into a well, and it is the only way containment survives a screen where figure and ground are the same material.
  • A rule primitive: ui_rule(rect, weight, tone), axes weight (hair | head) and orientation (h | v). There are five rules on this page doing the structural work boxes do elsewhere. Today each is a hand-placed DrawRectangleRec whose 1px-vs-2px decision lives at the call site. The weight is the hierarchy signal on a page, so it belongs in the component.
  • A ruled table: ui_table(rect, columns) where a column is { x_offset, align }, axes column count and align (left | right). The heading row and all four entries must agree on three x-offsets; here they agree because I typed 338 / 620 / 1174 five times. One column spec handed to a heading proc and a row proc is the difference between a table and five coincidences.
  • Right-aligned text placement: ui_text_right(rect, text). The style guide already records the missing measure-then-centre helper. A price column needs the right-aligned twin, and the letterhead needs it too. Digits that do not right-align are the reason a bill stops reading as a bill.
  • An availability axis on the row proc: affordable | unaffordable. Dimming is currently three coordinated edits — face shade, ink tone, rule tone. It should be one enum that resolves to a colour_shade factor and an ink pair, so no future screen can dim two of the three and ship it.
  • A button with a face axis: outlined | raised | tab. "Controls are outlined over a translucent ground" was written for controls over the world. On a full parchment field an outlined parchment button has no ground to separate from, and Leave is the weakest thing on this mockup as a direct result. tab is the same component with its leading edge suppressed, because it grows out of a panel edge — which is exactly what the Chart tab is now.
  • The crate mark as a shape: ui_glyph_crate(rect). Drawn here as a bordered box, and it must stay a primitive — the style guide's Glyphs are shapes, not text rule. It appears four times on this screen and will appear on every screen that shows a price.

One thing I did not ask for: a third type size. The page is large enough that a 32px letterhead and 16px everywhere else carries it, with colour doing the rest — which is the house rule, and the constraint did not break it.
